# Ralli Campus Ambassador Program & First-Campus Launch Playbook

## The core idea

Ralli only works once a real, connected friend group is using it together — a stranger opening the app to an empty feed churns immediately, while a friend group that joins together has an immediate reason to keep opening it. So the goal of this program is not "maximize downloads." It's: get one dense, real social graph fully onto the app and genuinely sticky before touching a second one.

Cornell is the first campus, because you're already embedded there.

---

## Part 1: The Ambassador Program

### Who to recruit

Not the person with the biggest following — the person who already has standing trust over a real group. That trust is what does the work; the app just needs to be good enough to keep them there once they show up.

Target profiles:
- Fraternity/sorority social chairs or presidents
- Club/org presidents (especially clubs with weekly in-person meetups — those already generate the kind of hourly "here's what we're doing" content Ralli is built for)
- Sports team captains
- RAs (built-in floor-wide trust)
- The friend-group's de facto planner — every group has one

Aim for 5-10 ambassadors for the first campus push. Each one is responsible for their own real group of 10-30 people, not for broad promotion to strangers. A small, high-touch program beats a large, low-touch one at this stage.

### What each ambassador gets (the "launch kit")

1. **A one-page explainer** — what Ralli is (hourly logs to your real friends, not followers), what it isn't (not another public feed, not Instagram/BeReal), and why it fits their specific group (team, house, club).
2. **2-3 example Daily Recaps** — real or seeded content showing what a week of logging looks like, so they have something concrete to show their group rather than describing it cold.
3. **A suggested first-pitch script** for their group chat — short, casual, framed as "we're all trying this for a week" rather than a hard sell.
4. **A direct line to you and Jordan** — ambassadors should feel like they're in on building this, not just distributing it. That access is itself part of the incentive.

### What each ambassador is actually asked to do

Not "get downloads." The concrete target is: **get your group logging to each other for 7 straight days.** Retention of one real group in week one matters far more than raw install count — a group that's still active on day 7 is the proof this works; a group that installed and stopped on day 2 is not a win even if it counts as 20 downloads.

### Compensation — reward activity, not installs

Avoid a per-signup bounty. Bounty-per-install schemes reliably produce fake or one-and-done installs, which for a network-effect product is actively worse than no ambassador at all — it pollutes your retention numbers and wastes the ambassador's real leverage on their group.

Instead: a flat stipend or Ralli merch/credit tied to their group's actual week-over-week logging activity (e.g. "your group logged at least 4 out of 7 days = you're paid/get merch"). This keeps the ambassador's incentive aligned with what actually matters: a group that sticks.

## Part 2: Week-by-Week Launch Checklist (First Campus)

### Week -2: Recruit and prep

- Identify and personally reach out to 8-12 candidate ambassadors (aim to land 5-10). Personal outreach from you or Jordan, not a form — this program depends on real relationships, so it should start as one.
- Build the launch kit (explainer, example recaps, pitch script).
- Pick the beachhead: one dorm, one Greek house, or one club — not "Cornell" broadly. Smaller and denser is stronger than wide and thin.
- Confirm the app is stable and any known blocking bugs (notifications, camera, overlay positioning — whatever's still open at that point) are resolved before you put it in front of real users. A rough first impression during a synchronized launch is much more costly than a rough impression on a slow trickle of individual signups.

### Week -1: Brief the ambassadors

- Walk every ambassador through the app live, not just the written kit — have them actually use it for a few days themselves first, so they're pitching from real experience.
- Set the synchronized launch date: all ambassadors push their group to join within the same 48-hour window. Simultaneity matters — "everyone's doing this right now" is real social pressure that a staggered rollout doesn't create.
- Set expectations clearly: the target is 7-day group retention, not signups.

### Week 1: Launch

- All ambassadors pitch their group and get everyone installed within the 48-hour window.
- You and Jordan are on call the entire week for bug reports — a synchronized launch means bugs get discovered by many people at once; fast response matters more than usual here.
- Track daily: how many of each group's members opened the app, how many logged at least once, how many are still active by day 7. This is your only real success metric for the week — ignore total install count as a vanity number.

### Week 2: Evaluate, don't expand yet

- Compare groups: which ambassador's group actually stuck? Talk to both the ones that worked and the ones that didn't — the reasons a group churned are the most useful information you'll get this month.
- Fix whatever the retention data or direct feedback surfaces before adding a second group. Expanding before the first network is proven is the most common way this kind of seeding fails — you end up with several thin, unstuck networks instead of one solid one.
- Pay out ambassador compensation based on actual week-over-week activity, as promised.

### Week 3-4: Second wave, same campus

- Once at least a few groups show real day-7+ retention, recruit a second wave of ambassadors from adjacent groups (friends of the groups that stuck spread the fastest — this is where the earlier growth-loop features matter: a shareable Daily Recap or an open Beacon from a group that's already active becomes free exposure to their followers who aren't onboarded yet).
- Keep expanding within Cornell before considering a second campus. Prove density in one place first.

### Beyond week 4: Second campus

- Only once Cornell shows a genuinely sticky core (not just total users — retained, actively-logging groups), repeat the same playbook at a second campus, incorporating whatever you learned from Cornell's launch about what pitch, incentive structure, and beachhead size actually worked.

---

## Guardrails — what not to do

- Don't pay per install. Pay for retained activity.
- Don't run this across many campuses at once before the first one is proven.
- Don't ask ambassadors to promote to strangers — the whole mechanic depends on pitching to a group they already have real trust with.
- Don't launch a synchronized push while there's a known bug that breaks a core loop (camera, sending, notifications) — a bad first 48 hours with a dense group is much harder to recover from than a slow trickle of individual signups would be.
